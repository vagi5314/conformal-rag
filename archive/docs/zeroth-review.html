AI lies. Confidently.

Large Language Models like ChatGPT generate plausible-sounding answers that are completely made up. This is called a hallucination. In healthcare or finance, a wrong answer can mean money lost or a patient harmed. There is no guarantee they won't.

A lie detector
with math

We use conformal prediction — a proven statistical framework — to guarantee that at most α% of accepted answers are hallucinations. The system abstains ("I don't know") whenever it's not confident enough.

NLI scorer (fast, free) LLM judge (precise, paid) CRC engine (guarantee)
P(hallucination | not abstained) ≤ α
"Of all answers we accept, at most α% are wrong"

What's
new here

Four contributions that fill gaps no existing tool addresses. This is the first production-ready conformal RAG library.

1. FDR-Guaranteed Detection

First production CRC implementation for RAG hallucination FDR. Not coverage — actual hallucination rate bound. "This answer has ≤5% chance of being wrong."

2. Hybrid NLI + LLM Scoring

Two-stage scorer: fast DeBERTa-v3 NLI (free, catches 70%) + optional LLM judge via LiteLLM (catches subtle 30%). 70% cost reduction.

3. Per-Domain Calibration

Topic-specific thresholds. Finance: α=0.01 (1% max error). Medical: α=0.001 (0.1% max error). No other tool does this.

4. Online Adaptive Update

ACI adjusts thresholds on streaming data. Handles distribution drift when your data changes over time (Gibbs & Candes, 2021).
