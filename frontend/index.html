Each topic keeps its own promise

A wrong dose in medicine costs more than a wrong ratio in finance, so the two topics do not share a tolerance. Each one carries its own bound, its own threshold and its own calibration set. The router sends a question to the topic it resembles most.

How many samples a tolerance needs

The finite sample bound needs enough calibration points to make the promise at all. Below that count the threshold sits above every score and the system withholds everything.

A topic is flagged for drift when the observed rate of approved and wrong answers rises above one and a half times its tolerance. That is a prompt to re-calibrate, not proof of harm.

What the guarantee says, and what it does not

One promise is stated, and it is the only one the numbers support. Everything else in this console is a consequence of it.

The promise

E[ P(approved and wrong) ] ≤ α

Read it as a rate over answers, not as a property of any single answer. If the system approves one thousand answers at a tolerance of 0.15, about one hundred and fifty of them are expected to be wrong, and the rest are right.

What it is not

It is not a promise that an approved answer is correct. It is also not the stricter claim that the share of wrong answers among approved ones stays under the tolerance. That conditional claim is not reachable by this method, and saying otherwise would overstate the result.

Source: When Can CRC Certify LLM Outputs?, 2026

How the threshold is set

The threshold is the lowest cut whose measured loss on the calibration set stays under the tolerance. The method is Conformal Risk Control, which extends conformal prediction from coverage to a bound on expected loss.

Source: Bates and others, Conformal Risk Control, 2021

Why answers are withheld

Withholding is the mechanism, not a failure. An answer under the threshold cannot be covered by the promise, so the honest move is to hand it back to a person instead of approving it. Raising the tolerance approves more answers and weakens the promise.

How an answer is scored

  1. 1

    Retrieve

    The question selects source passages from a hybrid dense and keyword index.

  2. 2

    Score faithfulness

    A natural language inference model scores whether the answer follows from the passage. A language model judge takes over when the score lands in the uncertain band and a provider key is configured.

  3. 3

    Compare to the threshold

    A score above the threshold is approved. Anything else is withheld and returned with its score.
